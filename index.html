§1 Quick summary

Before the legal detail, the short version: IDection Player has no servers of its own and no user account. It plays files that are already on your phone, on your local network, or on a server you set up yourself. These are the only places outside your phone the app ever sends anything to, and why:

We add no analytics or advertising of our own, there is no cross-app tracking, and we sell data to no one. The only diagnostics sent are produced by Google's own libraries — the Cast SDK when you cast, and ML Kit when you translate lyrics — and they never reach us. Everything else — your favorites, your usage history, your saved connectors — lives only on your phone.

§3 Data we collect and why

Credentials for your network connectors

When you add an SMB, WebDAV, FTP, or local folder connection, we store the alias, host, port, remote folder, and the username/password you enter. They are stored encrypted in the operating system's secure storage (Android Keystore), only on your phone. They are used solely to connect to THAT server when you choose to play something from it — they never leave your local network toward us or any third party.

Subtitles

Subtitles come from your own files: the ones already inside the video, a .srt file with the same name next to it, or a file you pick yourself from your device. The app does not search for or download subtitles from any service, so nothing about the video you are watching — not its name, not a fingerprint of it — is sent anywhere. Subtitles you load stay only on your device.

Usage data, on the device

To show you rankings such as "Most used receivers" or "Tracks played", we store locally which connectors, DLNA/Chromecast receivers, and tracks you used, and how often. This information is kept in a local database (Isar) on your device and is never transmitted to any server — not even ours, because we don't have one.

Song lyrics

The lyrics you see come from the song file itself: a .lrc file with the same name next to it, or the lyrics saved in the file's own tags. The app does not ask any service for lyrics, so the title, the artist or anything else about what you are listening to is never sent anywhere. They are read on your phone, and we do not store them.

Lyrics translation (Google ML Kit)

If you turn on translation in the lyrics view, the lyrics are translated on your phone with Google's ML Kit library: the text of the lyrics is not sent to any server for translation. The first time you translate from or into a language, ML Kit downloads that language's model (about 30 MB) from Google.

As Google documents, that library, not IDection Player, sends diagnostics and usage data to Google: information about the phone (manufacturer, model, Android version), the app's package name and version, per-installation identifiers that are not intended to identify you, performance metrics, error codes, and, for this feature, the source and destination languages and the language it detected. Google says it encrypts this data in transit and does not transfer it to third parties. We never receive it. If you never turn on translation, none of this is sent. Details: ML Kit data disclosure. The target language you choose is stored only on your phone.

Casting logs (Google Cast SDK)

To stream to Chromecast devices, the app uses Google's Cast SDK. That library, not IDection Player, sends anonymized logs to Google about how casting works: device discovery events, casting session events, general information about the phone, and which app is casting. According to Google, these logs contain no identifiers that can be traced back to you, are encrypted in transit, are used only to improve the casting experience, and are kept for a short period before being aggregated. We never receive them, and neither we nor you can turn them off or request their deletion. Details: Cast SDK data disclosure.

Notification access: what we do NOT read

The app asks for Android's "Notification access" for two specific purposes, and it is worth being explicit because it is a broad permission: Android requires it to be enabled in order to see which other apps are playing something (the only way to offer playback control of other apps, in the Streaming module), and the same access lets the app silence alerts on demand, when you turn on Do Not Disturb in the app, until you turn it off or the chosen time without playback runs out.

What the app does with this access is limited by design: we do not read, store, or transmit the content of any notification. Technically, the service does not implement the methods that receive notifications — not from messaging apps, not from email, not from anything else. You can revoke this access at any time in Android settings, and the rest of the app keeps working.

Status of your Bluetooth devices

The "Bluetooth audio" screen reads from the system which audio device is connected, with which codec, at which volume, and with how much battery, in order to show it to you. This information is read on the spot and is neither stored nor transmitted. With the Plus add-on you can also ask the system to use a different codec; that request never leaves the phone. We do not access any other data from your paired devices, nor your contacts, nor messages.

Diagnostics log

We keep, locally and only on your phone, a technical log of errors and of some of the app's internal decisions — for example, a playback error, which connector or subtitle was active at that moment, or whether a video was sent as-is or converted before sending it to Chromecast. It may include file or folder names and the alias of your connectors, as well as the app and Android versions — never your passwords. It is visible under Settings → Diagnostics log, and it is never sent anywhere on its own.

If you decide to share it (the "Share" button on that same screen), Android's native share sheet opens so you can choose where to send it — email, WhatsApp, or any other app you have installed. You make that submission yourself, choosing the destination; we do not receive it automatically, and the app or service you choose to send it with is governed by its own privacy policy, not ours. The same screen shows the email address you can send it to if you would like us to review it.

What we do NOT do

  • We add no analytics, advertising, or tracking SDKs. The only diagnostics sent are those of Google's own libraries described above — the Cast SDK when you cast, and ML Kit when you translate lyrics — which we never receive.
  • We do not access your location — the network permission used to discover Chromecast/DLNA devices is explicitly declared to Android as "never used for location".
  • We do not read the content of your notifications, even with the access enabled (see above).
  • We do not read, index, or upload the content of your audio/video files to any server.
  • We do not sell data to anyone, under any circumstances.

§4 Who data is shared with

Only with these destinations, and only when you use that specific feature:

  • The server you set up (your NAS, your PC with a shared folder, your DLNA server) — it receives the credentials you entered yourself, so it can authenticate you.
  • Google (ML Kit) — only if you turn on lyrics translation, it receives the diagnostics described in §3, collected directly by Google's library, and serves the language models. The lyrics themselves are translated on your phone.
  • Google Play (Google LLC) — it processes the purchase of the Plus add-on through Google Play Billing. We only receive confirmation that the purchase exists, never your payment method or banking details. Each time the app starts it also asks Google Play, on your phone, whether a newer version is available, and Google Play offers to download it; this uses Google Play's in-app updates service and sends no data of ours.
  • Google (Google Cast SDK) — it receives the anonymized casting logs described in §3, collected directly by Google's library. They are not shared with us or with anyone else.
  • Whoever you choose, when sharing the diagnostics log — the app opens Android's native share sheet and you decide the destination (email, WhatsApp, or another installed app). It is not an automatic submission on our part; see the details in §3.
Chromecast and Google Cast are trademarks of Google LLC. This app uses the public Google Cast SDK to stream to Chromecast devices on your local network. This app is not affiliated with, sponsored by, or endorsed by Google.

The details are in §3, and you can revoke it whenever you want without the rest of the app stopping working.

§6 Security

Every credential (network connectors) is stored encrypted using flutter_secure_storage, backed by the operating system's own Android Keystore — the same mechanism banking apps use to protect sensitive data on the device. They are never stored in plain text, nor in the local database, nor in backup files.

§7 Retention and how to delete your data

Since there is no account and no server of our own, all your data lives on your device. You can delete it yourself at any time, without having to contact us:

  • A single connector: tap the delete icon next to that connection, in the Connectors section.
  • The cache of downloaded files: the "Cache" card on the Home screen, "Clear now" button.
  • The diagnostics log: the delete icon under Settings → Diagnostics log.
  • Everything at once: uninstalling the app deletes absolutely all of the above — nothing remains on any server, because there was never a copy outside your phone.

§8 Purchases: the Plus add-on

IDection Player works fully and without ads in its free mode: all audio, video on the phone with your own subtitles, the equalizer, local folders, and one DLNA library and one DLNA receiver.

The Plus add-on is a single, lifetime purchase — it is not a subscription and it is never charged on a recurring basis. It unlocks: streaming to Chromecast, sending video to a network receiver, network connectors (SMB/WebDAV/FTP/FTPS), switching the Bluetooth codec, publishing this device as a DLNA receiver or library, and using more than one DLNA library and receiver.

It is processed entirely through Google Play Billing; you can restore your purchase on any device where you sign in with the same Google account, using the "Restore purchase" button on the Plus screen.

§9 Children

IDection Player is not directed at children under 13 and does not knowingly collect data from children.

As explained in §3, the app has no server of its own: we keep no copy of credentials anywhere we can access. So if a child in your care used the app, the most effective step is not to write to us, but rather:

  • Credentials for a network connector (SMB/WebDAV/FTP): they live encrypted only on that phone. Uninstalling the app deletes them completely, or you can change the password of that server directly.

If instead you wrote to us directly (for example, by email, sharing personal information), we can delete that specific communication on request: skrvaxdev@gmail.com.
